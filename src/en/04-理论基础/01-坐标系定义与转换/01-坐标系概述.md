# Coordinate Systems Overview

## What Is a Coordinate System

A coordinate system is a mathematical framework used to describe the position and orientation of points, vectors, or objects in space. In aerospace mission analysis and design, coordinate systems are the fundamental tools for expressing orbital states, attitudes, and relative geometric relationships.

A complete coordinate system consists of three core elements:

- **Origin**: The reference point from which coordinates are measured, typically the center of mass of a celestial body, a spacecraft, the Solar System Barycenter (SSB), or a point on the Earth's surface.
- **Axes**: A set of mutually orthogonal unit vectors that define the spatial reference directions, generally following the right-hand rule.

::: tip Reference Frame vs. Coordinate System
In precision astrodynamics applications, a *Reference Frame* refers to a celestial reference system realized by a set of precisely measured fiducial sources (e.g., extragalactic radio sources, stars). A *Coordinate System* is the mathematical description framework built upon that reference frame. The ICRF (International Celestial Reference Frame) is a typical example of the former.
:::

## Classification of Coordinate Systems

In ATK, coordinate systems are classified into the following major categories based on application scenarios:

### Inertial Frames

Inertial frames are coordinate systems that do not rotate with time (no rotational acceleration) in the Newtonian sense, typically centered at the Solar System barycenter or the center of a celestial body. They are used to describe the absolute motion of celestial bodies and form the basis for the validity of dynamical equations.

Common inertial frames include:

- **ICRF** (International Celestial Reference Frame) — Defined by the IAU and realized by the positions of distant extragalactic radio sources, it is the preferred inertial reference for astrometry and space missions.
- **GCRF** (Geocentric Celestial Reference Frame) — The geocentric realization of the ICRF, aligned with the ICRF through a pure translation (no rotation), serving as the standard inertial reference for all Earth satellite orbit computations.
- **J2000** (J2000.0 Mean Equator and Equinox Frame) — Referenced to the Earth's mean equator and mean equinox at the J2000.0 epoch (JD 2451545.0 TDB), commonly used in classical dynamics.
- **Inertial frames of various central bodies** (e.g., `Mars.Inertial`, `Moon.Inertial`) — These maintain exactly the same axis orientations as ICRF (no rotation), with only the origin translated to the corresponding body's center of mass, serving as the inertial reference for dynamics analysis of that body.

### Body-Fixed Frames

Frames rigidly attached to a central body, rotating with it, used to describe positions and directions relative to the body's surface. These frames are related to their corresponding inertial frames via Earth Orientation Parameters (such as polar motion, rotation angle, etc.).

Common body-fixed frames include:

- **Fixed** — The IAU-recommended realization of a body-fixed frame (e.g., `Earth.Fixed` based on ITRF, `Moon.Fixed_IAU2003` based on the IAU 2003 lunar orientation model).
- **TrueOfDate** — Defined by the instantaneous true equator and true equinox at the current epoch, including nutation effects.
- **MeanOfDate** — Defined by the mean equator and mean equinox at the current epoch, including precession but excluding nutation.

### Trajectory-Based Frames

Frames defined based on a spacecraft's orbital motion, commonly used for describing relative orbital geometry, formation flying, rendezvous and docking, relative navigation, and similar applications. These frames require a reference object (central body, spacecraft, etc.).

Common trajectory-based frames include:

- **LVLH** (Local Vertical Local Horizontal) — x-axis from the central body center to the spacecraft center of mass (radially outward), y-axis along the spacecraft velocity direction, z-axis along the orbital angular momentum direction, forming a right-handed system.
- **VVLH** (Vehicle Velocity Local Horizontal) — z-axis toward the central body center, y-axis along the negative normal of the orbital plane, x-axis completing the right-handed system (approximately along-track).
- **RIC** (Radial-InTrack-CrossTrack) — R-axis along the radial direction (from central body to spacecraft), I-axis along the flight direction (in-track), C-axis along the orbital plane normal, forming a right-handed system.
- **VNC** (Velocity-Normal-CoNormal) — V-axis along the velocity direction, N-axis along the orbital plane normal, C-axis completing the right-handed system (co-normal).

### Surface-Based Frames

Frames defined with respect to a reference point on the surface of a central body (e.g., longitude, latitude, altitude), commonly used for describing ground stations, landing sites, target points, and local geometric relationships.

Common surface-based frames include:

- **NorthEastDown (NED)** — x-axis points north, y-axis points east, z-axis points toward the center of the body (down), widely used in aerospace ground station applications.
- **EastNorthUp (ENU)** — x-axis points east, y-axis points north, z-axis points to the zenith (up), the most common right-handed frame in civilian navigation and robotics.
- **TopoCentric** — Origin at an observation station, fundamental plane is the local horizontal plane, z-axis points to the zenith, x-axis points north or east depending on convention.
- **RadialCentric** — Origin at an arbitrary point, z-axis along the radial direction from the body center outward (zenith), x-axis points north in the local horizontal plane, y-axis points east, used for spacecraft surface component mounting and planetary lander local navigation.

### Epoch-Based Frames

Frames defined at a specific epoch, aligned with the corresponding time-varying frame at that epoch, but then frozen (i.e., no longer rotating with time). Widely used in TLE/SGP4 orbit propagation and high-precision orbit integration.

Common epoch-based frames include:

- **AlignmentAtEpoch** — Aligned with a reference frame at a specified epoch, then remains fixed thereafter.
- **MeanOfEpoch** — Frozen after aligning with MeanOfDate at a specified epoch.
- **TrueOfEpoch** — Frozen after aligning with TrueOfDate at a specified epoch.
- **TEMEOfEpoch** (True Equator Mean Equinox of Epoch) — At the specified epoch, the equator is the instantaneous true equator and the equinox is the mean equinox; this is the standard output frame for the SGP4/SDP4 orbit propagation models.

### Ecliptic Frames

Frames referenced to the ecliptic plane (the plane of Earth's orbit around the Sun), available in both geocentric and heliocentric versions, commonly used in interplanetary missions, deep-space exploration, and solar system dynamics analysis.

Common ecliptic frames include:

- **MeanEclpJ2000** (J2000.0 Mean Ecliptic Frame) — Defined by the mean ecliptic plane and mean equinox at J2000.0, with origin either geocentric or heliocentric; this is the most commonly used ecliptic frame in aerospace engineering.
- **TrueEclpDate** (True Ecliptic Frame of Date) — Defined by the instantaneous true ecliptic plane and true equinox at the specified epoch, including full precession and nutation effects.
- **J2000_Ecliptic** — A common alias for MeanEclpJ2000, using the mean ecliptic plane at J2000.0 as the reference plane, x-axis pointing to the J2000.0 mean equinox, with origin either geocentric or heliocentric.

## Naming Conventions for Coordinate Systems

In ATK, coordinate system names generally follow this pattern:

```
[CentralBody].[CoordinateSystemType]
```

For example:
- `Earth.Fixed` — Earth-fixed coordinate system
- `Moon.MeanEarth` — Moon mean Earth coordinate system
- `Satellite1.LVLH` — LVLH trajectory-based frame for Satellite1

## Guidelines for Selecting Coordinate Systems

Choosing the appropriate coordinate system is crucial for different mission analysis scenarios:

| Application Scenario | Recommended Frame | Rationale |
|----------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Describing absolute orbital state | ICRF / GCRF or J2000 | Inertial reference, simple dynamical equations |
| Describing position relative to Earth's surface | Earth.Fixed | Fixed to surface features, facilitates geolocation |
| Formation flying relative motion analysis | LVLH or RIC | CW equations are concise in LVLH |
| Rendezvous and docking relative navigation | LVLH or VVLH | Intuitively reflects approach corridor and braking directions |
| Ground station visibility and observation geometry | NorthEastDown or TopoCentric | Facilitates azimuth and elevation computations |
| Interplanetary transfer trajectory design | MeanEclpJ2000 or ICRF | Motion primarily in ecliptic plane, ΔV assessment intuitive |
| Lunar surface missions | Moon.MeanEarth or Moon.Fixed_IAU2003 | IAU-recommended lunar orientation models |
| TLE orbit propagation | TEMEOfEpoch | Standard frame for SGP4/SDP4 |
| Attitude determination and control | Inertial frame consistent with orbit integration | Ensures unified reference |
| Deep-space probe navigation | ICRF | Consistent with DSN tracking data processing reference frame |

## Overview of Relationships Among Common Coordinate Systems

```
ICRF (International Celestial Reference Frame, quasi-inertial)
  │
  ├── GCRF (Geocentric Celestial Reference Frame, translation without rotation from ICRF)
  │     │
  │     ├── J2000 (Geocentric inertial, with only a small constant frame bias from GCRF: ~17 mas)
  │     │
  │     ├── Earth.Inertial (Fixed rotation relative to GCRF, i.e., Earth orientation reference)
  │     │     │
  │     │     ├── Earth.Fixed (ITRF, rotating with Earth)
  │     │     │     ├── TrueOfDate (instantaneous equator + instantaneous equinox)
  │     │     │     ├── MeanOfDate (mean equator + mean equinox, precession only)
  │     │     │     └── TEMEOfDate (instantaneous equator + mean equinox)
  │     │     │
  │     │     └── TrueOfEpoch / MeanOfEpoch / TEMEOfEpoch (frozen at a specified epoch)
  │     │
  │     └── Trajectory-based frames (bound to specific spacecraft objects)
  │           ├── LVLH
  │           ├── RIC / ICR
  │           ├── VNC
  │           └── VVLH
  │
  ├── Inertial frames of other central bodies (e.g., Mars.Inertial, Moon.Inertial)
  │     └── Corresponding Fixed / TrueOfDate / MeanOfDate for that body
  │
  ├── Ecliptic frames
  │     ├── MeanEclpJ2000
  │     └── TrueEclpDate
  │
  └── Surface-based frames
        ├── NorthEastDown / NorthWestUp
        ├── TopoCentric
        └── RadialCentric
```

::: warning Note
- The hierarchical relationships above represent conceptual derivation and transformation chains. Actual mathematical transformations may involve multiple intermediate steps (e.g., sequential products of polar motion, rotation, nutation, and precession matrices).
- J2000 and GCRF/ICRF have a frame bias of approximately 17 milliarcseconds, which is non-negligible for high-precision applications.
- For specific transformation methods, refer to [Coordinate Transformation Methods](./06-坐标转换方法.md).
:::
