# Time Constraints

This chapter gives the definitions and conversion relationships of the time bases involved in Temporal Constraints.

## 1 Local Time

<a id="local-time"></a>

Local Time (LT) is measured in the wall-clock time of the object's time zone, that is, Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) plus the fixed offset of that time zone:

$$
LT=UTC+\Delta_{\mathrm{zone}}
$$

where $\Delta_{\mathrm{zone}}$ is the offset of the object's time zone relative to UTC (positive east of Greenwich). When the local mean time is determined from the longitude of the object, the offset is derived from the longitude:

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{zone}}=\frac{\lambda}{15^{\circ}}(\mathrm{h})
$$

where $\lambda$ is the longitude of the object (positive east of Greenwich), and $15^{\circ}$ of longitude corresponds to a time difference of 1 h. At the same instant, objects in different time zones have different local times; the Local constraint therefore bounds the access window by the object's local working hours.

## 2 Greenwich Mean Time

<a id="gmt"></a>

Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the mean solar time referred to the prime meridian ($0^{\circ}$ longitude).

## 3 Local Apparent Time

<a id="local-apparent-time"></a>

Local Apparent Time (LAT) is measured by the position of the Sun relative to the local meridian; it is also called local apparent solar time.
