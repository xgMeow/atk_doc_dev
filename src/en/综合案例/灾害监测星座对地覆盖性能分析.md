# Coverage Performance Analysis of a Disaster Monitoring Constellation

## Introduction

The Disaster Monitoring Constellation (DMC) consists of multiple Earth-observation satellites that monitor ground targets with long duration and high precision by means of relayed observation and joint observation of targets among satellites. Its monitoring targets include already-disaster-affected areas or disaster-prone regions, and its purpose is to provide data support for disaster early warning, emergency response, and post-disaster assessment by quickly acquiring image information about the relevant areas. Analyzing and evaluating the coverage performance of a disaster monitoring constellation is of great significance for constellation mission scheduling, constellation effectiveness evaluation, and constellation layout optimization.

![Schematic of the Walker constellation observing the Earth](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image01-Walker星座对地观测示意图.png)

A Walker constellation refers to a uniformly distributed satellite constellation centered on the Earth that is composed of multiple circular-orbit satellites with the same orbital altitude and orbital inclination. That is, the right ascensions of the ascending node of all orbital planes are uniformly distributed in $[0, 2\pi]$, each orbital plane contains the same number of satellites, and the satellites in an orbital plane are also uniformly distributed. Because of its uniform distribution around the Earth, this constellation is the most effective constellation for global coverage and is commonly used for Earth observation, navigation, and Internet communication, among others<sup>[1]</sup>. In view of the good Earth-observation coverage of Walker constellations, this document refers to the international DMC constellation and the design of China's "36 Tiangang constellation", and uses the constellation design module of the ATK simulation platform to design a Walker constellation composed of 36 satellites as the Earth-observation constellation of this case (named the DMC constellation). Through the coverage analysis module, quality factors such as the N Asset Coverage and coverage time are computed to generate the corresponding figures, and the coverage performance of this constellation over global disaster-prone regions and the areas surrounding China is analyzed.

## Constellation Design and Earth-Coverage Analysis Methods

### Walker Constellation Model

The definition of the Walker constellation parameters is shown in the figure below, and the common description form is $(N/P/F, h, i)$, where $N$ is the total number of satellites in the constellation; $P$ is the number of orbital planes of the constellation; $F$ is the phase factor, which can be an integer between $0 \sim (P-1)$ and represents the phase relationship between the corresponding satellites of two adjacent orbital planes in a Walker constellation; the difference in right ascension of the ascending node between two adjacent orbital planes is $\Delta\Omega = 2\pi/P$; the phase difference between adjacent satellites in the same orbital plane is $\Delta\Phi = 2\pi/(N/P)$; the phase difference between satellites in different orbits of adjacent planes is $\Delta f = 2\pi F/N$; $h$ is the orbital altitude of the satellites in the constellation; and $i$ is the orbital inclination of the satellites in the constellation.

![Walker constellation parameter definition](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image04-Walker星座参数定义图.png)

In a Walker constellation identified as $N/P/F$, the right ascension of the ascending node $\Omega_{mn}$ and the argument of latitude $u_{mn}$ of the $n$-th satellite on the $m$-th orbital plane are respectively<sup>[2]</sup>:

$$
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{mn} &= \frac{360}{P} \left( P_m - 1 \right) \quad && (P_m = 1,2,\dots,P) \\
u_{mn} &= \frac{360}{N_S} \left( S_n - 1 \right) + \frac{360}{N} F \left( P_m - 1 \right) \quad && (S_n = 1,2,\dots,N_S)
\end{aligned}
\right.
$$

where $N_s$ is the number of satellites on each orbital plane, $N_s = N/P$.

### Satellite-to-Ground Coverage Model

The motion of the satellite and the rotation of the Earth cause the subsatellite point of the satellite to move over the Earth's surface, forming a subsatellite ground track. The satellite can observe a ground target only when it moves over the target, as shown in the figure below. When the target is within the coverage range of the satellite, the imaging payload of the satellite can observe the ground target, thus achieving coverage of the ground target.

![Schematic of satellite coverage of a ground target (a)](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image06-卫星对地面目标覆盖示意图a.png)

According to the definition of coverage, the satellite-ground spatial relationship shown in the figure below can be obtained. As long as the target is within the field of view of the satellite, the target can be considered covered by the satellite. The corresponding observation equation is given below. In the equation, $\lambda$ is the target angle, $\rho$ is the observation range angle, $\varepsilon$ is the field-of-view angle, $R_s$ is the satellite orbital radius, and $R_e$ is the radius of the Earth.

$$
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
\theta_T &< \theta_{FOV} \\
\theta_{FOV} &= \arcsin\left(\frac{\sin(\alpha_{FOV}) R_S}{R_E}\right) - \alpha_{FOV} \\
\theta_T &= \arccos\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{SE} \cdot \boldsymbol{r}_{TE}}{\|\boldsymbol{r}_{SE}\| \|\boldsymbol{r}_{TE}\|}\right)
\end{aligned}
\right.
$$

![Schematic of the satellite-ground spatial geometric relationship (b)](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image07-星地空间几何关系示意图b.png)

## Scenario Creation and Constellation Generation

1. **Create a new scenario**

    Install and run ATK.exe, and create a new scenario "Disaster Monitoring Constellation Coverage Analysis". Set the start epoch to 2024-04-13 00:00:00.000000 and the stop epoch to 2024-04-14 00:00:00.000000.

2. **Import the DMC constellation and sensors**

    Click the <kbd>Constellation Design</kbd> module in the【Tools】tab and set the constellation type to Walker-Delta; set the parameters of the seed satellite: semi-major axis 7064861 m, eccentricity 0, inclination 98.1287°, and right ascension of the ascending node, argument of perigee, and true anomaly all 0; set the constellation parameters: total number of satellites 36, number of orbital planes 12, and adjacent-plane phase factor 3. Click <kbd>Calculate</kbd>, and the orbital elements of each satellite are displayed in the window on the right as follows:

    ![DMC constellation orbital elements table](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-1.png)

    Click <kbd>Insert Satellites</kbd>, select all satellites in the object view, right-click and choose <kbd>Batch Insert</kbd>, select <kbd>Sensors</kbd>, and click <kbd>Insert</kbd>. In the "Group View" on the right, right-click the "Disaster Monitoring Constellation Coverage Analysis" scenario, choose <kbd>Create Group</kbd>, and name it "DMC". Move all the satellites and their sensors into this group.

    In the object view, select all the sensors, right-click and choose <kbd>Batch Property Edit</kbd>, set the【Sensor Type】to【Matrix】, and define the parameters:【Vert Half Angle】26.7° and【Hori Half Angle】15°<sup>[2]</sup>. The 2D and 3D views of the constellation are shown as follows:

    ![2D view of the constellation](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-2.png)

    ![3D view of the constellation](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-3.png)

## Import of Observation Targets and Regions

1. **Key monitoring regions (point targets)**

    Create a new group and name it "Key Monitoring Regions". Then select <kbd>Insert Object</kbd> - <kbd>Facility</kbd>, input the latitude and longitude of the collected disaster-prone locations, and insert them into this group.

    ::: note Note

    The facilities can be obtained by searching the latitude and longitude of relevant locations on the Internet, or by inserting facilities from the【City Database】. This case chooses the latter. In【City Database - Country】, search "CHILE", "MEXICO", and "INDONESIA", select【National Capital】as the【Type】, insert the facilities of the three cities, and rename them "Chile", "Mexico", and "Indonesia" in turn.

    :::

    ![Table of point targets in the key monitoring regions](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-4.png)

2. **Disaster-prone regions (large regions)**

    Here Mexico, Indonesia, Chile, and their surrounding sea areas are selected as the key observation regions. Select <kbd>Insert Object</kbd> - <kbd>Coverage Definition</kbd>, and modify the coverage definition properties: the "Grid Area of Interest" type is set to "Latitude-Longitude Area", and the "Grid Definition" latitude-longitude spacing is 1°. The latitude-longitude region parameters of Mexico, Indonesia, and Chile are as follows:

    ![Region parameter table of the disaster-prone areas](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image13-灾害频发地区区域参数表.png)

    The region grid points can be seen displayed in the 2D view as follows:

    ![2D view of the region grid points](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-5.png)

## Constellation Coverage Performance Analysis

### Point Target Coverage Analysis

Select any location to demonstrate the constellation coverage analysis operation. This document takes Chile as an example to carry out the coverage analysis. Right-click the location and select <kbd>Coverage</kbd>, as shown in the figure:

![Selecting coverage analysis by right-clicking](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-6.png)

Then select the sensors of all 36 satellites of the DMC constellation, and click <kbd>Calculate</kbd>, as shown in the figure below.

![Selecting the sensors for computation](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-7.png)

In【Quality Parameters】, click <kbd>Define...</kbd> to solve for different quality factors, as shown in the figure:

![Quality factor parameter table](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-9.png)

Select <kbd>N Asset Coverage</kbd> as the quality factor, and click <kbd>Coverage Intervals Curve</kbd> or other curves for display. The operation is shown in the figure:

![N Asset Coverage analysis operation](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-10.png)

The following Gantt chart can be obtained. It can be found that the coverage time over the Chile region is relatively short and there is no multi-layer coverage, but the constellation's coverage of this region is relatively uniform in time distribution, so that when a disaster occurs in the region, it can be detected within a relatively short time.

![Coverage Gantt chart](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-11.png)

### Region Target Coverage Analysis

Take the coverage analysis of the Mexico region as an example. In the object view, right-click the object "Mexico Monitoring Region", select the <kbd>Regional Coverage</kbd> function, select all the sensors, set the simulation time to 24 h and the orbit step to 60 s, and click <kbd>Calculate</kbd>. The simulation computation takes about 10 seconds.

![Regional coverage computation results](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-12.png)

After the simulation computation finishes, the related reports and figures can be viewed, such as the regional coverage percent report and figure shown below:

![Regional coverage percent report](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-13.png)

![Regional coverage percent figure](../../zh/综合案例/media/灾害监测星座对地覆盖性能分析/image0612-14.png)

From the regional coverage percent report and figure, the real-time coverage percentage and the accumulated coverage percentage at each simulation time can be obtained. It can be seen that after about 7 h the constellation achieves 100% coverage of the Mexico region. In addition, reports such as the "Partial Coverage Report" and the "Coverage By Assets Report" can also be viewed.
